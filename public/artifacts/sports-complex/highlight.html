What to Show a Prospect
  • 01 Revenue model tab (Excel, Sheet3). Walk through two or three line items to show how assumptions were built: "350 clients at $450/month" is a different conversation than showing a total. Prospects who understand numbers will ask about the assumptions — that's where the rigor lives, not in the gross revenue totals.
  • 02 Project Fees table (Proposal, page 9). The market-rate comparison is an immediate value signal: $1.1M market rate versus a $352K proposed fee. No explanation needed. It repositions the conversation from "what does this cost" to "what would this cost anywhere else."
  • 03 Milestone tables (Proposal, pages 6–8). The phased structure with specific start dates, durations, and named deliverables proves project management depth — not just a consulting approach, but an operating rhythm. Show a skeptical prospect that you manage against deadlines, not against hours billed.
  • 04 The Purpose section as a pull quote (Proposal, page 1). The whereas/therefore structure translates a founder's scattered vision into a clean, binding professional scope. This is the moment to say: "This is how I take what a client is trying to do and make it real on paper before we spend a dollar."
  • 05 Ownership model (Excel, Sheet2). Six investor tiers, per-share pricing at $20.51, and a $1M distribution model side-by-side. This shows deal structuring judgment — not just financial modeling, but understanding how equity gets carved up and communicated to investors with different contribution levels.
